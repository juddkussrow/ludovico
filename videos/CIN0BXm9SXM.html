The core innovation addresses the "stability paradox" of monowheels, which traditionally are unstable due to a single ground contact point. The solution involves a vertically oriented gyroscope that spins rapidly inside the robot, providing a moment of inertia to maintain upright stability. To enable steering, instead of trying to turn the vehicle directly, the robot tilts, causing the gyroscope's precession to rotate on a reciprocal axis, thus allowing the vehicle to turn. This patented methodology allows for both stability and maneuverability. ROLLO is presented as a cost-efficient platform designed to complement human labor, particularly in critical infrastructure security and surveillance. The robot can autonomously patrol perimeters, detect irregularities, and identify threats like animals, humans, vehicles, and fires, and read license plates. It then alerts security teams. The robot's design emphasizes efficiency, with a single touch point for low rolling resistance and high energy efficiency, leading to long battery life. Key components like motors, gyros, and IMUs are integrated within the wheel. The tire itself is custom-designed, thin but strengthened with carbon fiber, allowing for optimal battery placement at the bottom for a low center of gravity. The robot remains stable even when switched off. The development process involved extensive rapid prototyping and testing, with the breakthrough discovery of the vertical gyroscope and tilting mechanism occurring almost by accident after numerous failed attempts. The creators aim to make ROLLO an open platform, allowing developers and enthusiasts to create their own applications and use cases, such as gardening, snow shoveling, or trash collection.
