This video introduces ADHD, an open-source skill for AI coding agents that addresses the problem of AI models producing similar, anchored ideas. ADHD works by spawning isolated reasoning branches under different cognitive frames, then uses a separate critic to score, cluster, and deepen the top ideas while flagging potential traps. It aims to provide more diverse and novel solutions for complex engineering problems, offering a significant improvement over baseline single-pass AI responses.

The core issue is that language models write one word at a time, and early commitments steer subsequent output, even in "tree of thought" approaches where branches still share context. ADHD solves this by implementing a two-phase architecture: "diverge" and "focus." In the "diverge" phase, it spawns five or more completely isolated agent calls, each given a problem and a "cognitive frame" (a unique vantage point like "3am on-call engineer" or "speedrunner"). These branches operate without shared context, preventing anchoring. There are 15 built-in frames, and users can create custom ones with about five lines of code. In the "focus" phase, a separate "critic" agent, with opposite instructions, reads all generated ideas, scores them on novelty, viability, and fit, groups them, flags potential traps with reasons, and deepens the top three into build-ready sketches. The separation between generator and critic is mechanical, not a prompt-based request. A benchmark from the repo demonstrates ADHD's effectiveness: for a problem involving retry/timeout strategy for a hanging command-line tool, a baseline single-shot answer yielded four textbook patterns, while ADHD generated over 30 ideas from six frames. A key non-obvious idea was a "rage-quit button" to resubmit to a cheaper, faster model, an idea the baseline never considered. ADHD also flagged 20 seductive but broken ideas. Benchmarks show ADHD significantly improves breadth of ideas (9.0 vs 4.8), novelty (7.8 vs 2.7), and trap detection (9.5 vs 1.8) compared to a plain one-shot answer. While it won five of six problems, overall builder usefulness was a tie (7.7 vs 6.8), indicating it excels in ideation, not general improvement. Installation is a single command: `npx skills add UditAkhourii/adhd`, supporting Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Cline, Gemini CLI, Windsurf, and ~50 other agents. A standalone CLI (`npm install -g adhd-agent`) and TypeScript library are also available. The tool is recommended for high-stakes, open-ended problems like architecture design, API design, fuzzy debugging, and migration planning, but not for simple lookups or latency-critical tasks due to the cost of reloading context for each isolated branch.
