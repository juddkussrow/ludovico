These managed agents typically encompass three layers: the model, the harness (which calls models, picks tools, and manages context), and the runtime (the sandbox for execution, observability, and tracing). The provider owns the harness and runtime layers, and often the model as well. Two main "flavors" of managed agents are identified. Flavor one, exemplified by Claude Managed Agents, Gemini Agents, Agent Core, and LangChain Managed Deep Agents, involves the provider supplying the entire loop, where users send tasks and configuration without needing to write the loop or call the model directly. Flavor two, including Foundry Hosted Agents and Agent Runtime, allows users to bring their own loop (e.g., written with LangGraph or 80K) in a container, which the provider then hosts with per-session isolation, offering runtime services like tool execution and memory. Google and LangChain are noted for offering both flavors. The video highlights the economic motivations behind managed agents, noting that they are high-token products due to their long-running, asynchronous nature. Anthropic's Claude Managed Agents charge a session-hour fee ($0.08 per session-hour) in addition to token costs. A significant concern is vendor lock-in: model providers like Anthropic and Google (with Gemini) tie users to their specific models. Cloud providers like AWS and Azure, however, offer more model-interchangeable managed agents, but lock users into their runtime infrastructure. LangChain, as a framework company, aims for operational lock-in through its LangSmith product, where traces, evals, and deployments are managed. The video presents five questions to guide the decision between managed agents and self-hosting: 1) Do you need state that survives disconnects and resumes days later? (Go managed). 2) Do you need a server-side sandbox for code execution? (Go managed). 3) Is your agent's loop your product? (Self-host). 4) Can your data leave your infrastructure? 5) Do you need zero-day data retention? (If not, managed agents like Claude Managed Agents are ruled out due to server-side session and sandbox state).
