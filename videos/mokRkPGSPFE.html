The technique involves bouncing light off a white or unbleached cloth placed as walls around the camera, creating a "cove" that wraps around the subject. Lights are placed on the floor, shooting upwards towards the cloth, with varying intensities (e.g., 100%, 30%, 5%) to create a soft falloff and subtle three-dimensionality. The setup is quick to build, using two stands, a pole, and clamps. The creator compares this to a "reverse keylight" setup, where the stronger Fresnel light is pushed to the background and a black cloth is used for negative fill, which is more natural but less intentional. Further experiments include adding a Black ProMist filter, which softens the image but isn't a definitive improvement, and haze, which improves the look when graded, especially with the reverse cove. The video then compares the cove light to a softbox (specifically Aputure's biggest dome) and a photographic umbrella. The softbox without a honeycomb is similar to the reverse cove but with light from the top, which the creator prefers less. However, adding a honeycomb to the softbox drastically changes the look, making the subject pop more, and is considered the second favorite look. The photographic umbrella is deemed "kind of bad," being harsh, creating shine, spill, and shadows. The creator concludes that the cove light is the best all-rounder for commercials and ads, the softbox with honeycomb is a surprise runner-up for internet ads and podcasts, and the reverse cove with negative fill and haze is suitable for documentary work where a natural yet dramatic feel is desired. The haze is considered worth it for a soft, warm, artsy feel on short or indie films, but too much hassle for interviews, while the Pro Mist is recommended to be skipped.
