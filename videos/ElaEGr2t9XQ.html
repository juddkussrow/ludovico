Hookdeck offers two main products: the Event Gateway and Outpost. The Event Gateway acts as a specialized event bus for external events (like webhooks from Stripe, Shopify, Twilio), standardizing their reception, filtering, transformation, routing, queuing, alerting, and replay capabilities. It helps manage throughput during flash sales or high-volume events. Outpost is an open-source (Apache 2.0) project, also available as a managed service, designed for sending events. It allows publishers to declare tenants, configure destinations, and publish events with observability, delivery guarantees, and retries. Outpost supports publishing events directly to message buses like RabbitMQ, Kafka, Pub/Sub, SQS, AWS EventBridge, and the Hookdeck Event Gateway, treating webhooks as a transport mechanism rather than the sole event delivery method. The idea for Hookdeck originated from Alex's frustration with managing webhooks in an e-commerce context, where issues like delivery guarantees, ordering, and error recovery (e.g., dead-letter queues) were complex and poorly handled by existing solutions. He views webhooks as a "gateway drug to event-driven architecture," emphasizing the need for better tooling to manage asynchronous event processing. Hookdeck aims to provide a better developer experience, abstracting away much of the underlying complexity. The decision to open-source Outpost was driven by the belief that the consumer side of webhooks (receiving) presents a larger business opportunity, and encouraging event production through Outpost aligns with Hookdeck's overall goals. The managed version of Outpost runs the exact same Docker build as the open-source project, ensuring feature parity and offering users a choice between self-hosting and managed service based on operational burden. The video mentions a testimonial from Vercel's CEO, Guillermo Rauch, who noted an increase in webhook usage driven by LLM adoption and AI agents, creating new use cases for event data.
