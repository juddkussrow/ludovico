This is termed the "mere exposure effect" and forms the basis of the "format steal" strategy: adopting existing viral formats and inserting a brand. An example given is Buldak, a Korean instant ramen brand, which grew from 300,000 followers to 1.8 million on TikTok and achieved over 900 million views in 12 months by pivoting to food-related viral formats like spicy challenges. The second principle is the "curiosity trap," where content should trigger curiosity, disbelief, or identity recognition rather than leading with the product. The brain seeks to fill knowledge gaps, creating a "mental itch." For Stan, an all-in-one creator platform, an office tour video featuring the CEO's story garnered a million views on TikTok and 5 million on LinkedIn, generating 20 million views for his personal brand by focusing on viewer curiosity before introducing the product. The third principle states that "People don't buy the product, they buy identities," referencing the means-end chain theory. Brands should focus on the psychological values and emotional outcomes (e.g., feeling in control of one's life from a supplement like AG1) rather than just product attributes or functional consequences. The fourth principle emphasizes "credentials in your hooks," as social media is shallow and the brain takes shortcuts, accepting authority (e.g., "Thai chef," "Harvard student"). A Japanese restaurant saw views jump from 200-300 to 1.8 million and gained 300,000 followers in 3 months by using a chef in a professional kitchen as a credential in a viral format. The fifth principle is that "People don't share what they like, they share what makes them look good" (smart, funny, knowledgeable). Content should be easily explainable in one sentence and evoke emotions like humor or surprise to encourage sharing. Finally, the sixth principle is to "hijack the viewer's brain with stories." Stories engage the emotional process, reducing critical thinking. A successful video structure is Hook, Problem, Story, Payoff. Editing should be fast-paced, delivering new information constantly, and captions are crucial for dual processing and retention. The creator concludes that understanding human psychology is paramount for virality, not the product itself.
