The creator, Tamara, explains that the HydraJet uses a 70mm electric ducted fan (EDF) for propulsion. Its design incorporates two wings that lift it above the water, allowing it to hydroplane or glide on the surface. This differs from hydrofoils, whose wings remain submerged and can lose lift if they breach. The HydraJet's fixed wings provide passive lift without actuation, prioritizing simplicity for an RC boat. While an air rudder was tried, it proved unusable at low speeds, leading to the implementation of a water rudder for control at both high and low speeds. The boat runs on a 6S battery, with a 2,900 mAh battery being the largest size designed for it. The creator mentions using a JP Hobby full aluminum machined EDF for its sound, but the action footage features the more affordable Power Fun EDF, which sounds "vacuum-esque." The decision to use an EDF over a water propeller was driven by cost, simplicity, and the creator already owning a 50mm EDF, avoiding the need to buy additional waterproof hardware. The video also touches upon the HydraJet Mk1, a 3D-printed predecessor that was sold as kits before being discontinued due to fragility. The creator is now developing a carbon fiber version and has a year-long waiting list for the HydraJet.
