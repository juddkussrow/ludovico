Developed by Unseen Realities, with a CEO who worked on Apple Vision Pro and Meta's XR team, the URXR One features virtual see-through technology, offering a full brightness view of the real world with opaque digital screens. It boasts a 5K dual-eye display (2.5K per eye), a 90-degree field of view (double many AR glasses, and 20 degrees larger than Xreal and Google's Project Aura), and supports seven simultaneous screens. The glasses connect via a USB-C cord and can be powered by a magnetic accessory that attaches to a phone or the glasses' case, preventing phone battery drain. It offers virtual environments, similar to Apple Vision Pro, with 3-4 available at launch and plans for more, including a developer kit for custom environments. Prescription support includes built-in diopter adjustments and third-party lens compatibility. The glasses support 6 degrees of freedom (6 DoF) and SLAM, enabling 360/180-degree video viewing and potential Steam VR compatibility, which is a primary goal for the company post-launch, pending a third-party controller solution. Controls include buttons for re-centering screens and switching between four default screen sizes (close/small, far/small, far/large, close/large), and hand tracking for moving and resizing screens. The demo version outputted at 1080p, while the launch version will output at 1440p. Pros include the clear, bright world background with opaque digital screens, superior to traditional AR glasses that dim backgrounds or show transparent screens in bright conditions. The 90-degree field of view provides a massive screen size, ideal for multiple screens. Cons are the lack of built-in speakers, requiring external headphones, and currently imperfect hand tracking, though the company is actively seeking feedback for improvement. The futuristic appearance might not appeal to those seeking glasses that look "normal." The URXR One is priced at $799, with a pre-order discount of $699 and a retail launch price of $899.
