This video explains how geocells, a 3D cellular confinement system, improve ground stability and strength for various engineering applications. It demonstrates their effectiveness in preventing bearing capacity failure and washboarding on unpaved roads through real-world examples and a custom-built model. The video highlights geocells as an environmentally beneficial alternative to traditional methods, reducing the need for expensive materials and extensive excavation.

Geocells are described as 3D structures made from plastic strips welded into a honeycomb shape, which encapsulate and confine soil, preventing shear failure and increasing bearing capacity. This is contrasted with traditional methods like expensive concrete footings or subgrade replacement with select-fill, and other geosynthetics like geotextiles (which lack strength and engagement with soil) and geogrids (which are 2D and less effective for thicker layers). The geocells, printed in flexible TPU, allow for slight bulging, engaging the material's strength and passive resistance of neighboring cells. The creator notes that while the geocells were placed at the top for visibility, they would normally be under a layer of cover. The video emphasizes the environmental benefits of geocells, including reduced excavation, use of local and cheaper base materials, decreased haul distances, and potential for more permeable surfaces. The technology was pioneered by the US Army Corps of Engineers in the 1970s and is even being researched by NASA for lunar applications.
