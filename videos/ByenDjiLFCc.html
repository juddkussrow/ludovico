Standard silicon solar panels convert only 15-22% of sunlight into electricity, with the remaining 80% becoming heat, which degrades electrical efficiency (losing 10-15% of rated power on hot days). PVT systems address this by actively removing heat from the solar cells, which not only provides hot water or heating but also increases the electrical output. The video highlights two European companies leading in PVT development: Naked Energy and Triple Solar. Naked Energy, founded in 2009 in the UK, developed the Virtu PVT system, which uses horizontal glass vacuum tubes containing monocrystalline cells. This design minimizes heat loss, allowing the system to achieve an estimated overall efficiency of 80% (20% electricity, 60% heat). Their tubes cover over 85% of the roof area due to their low-profile design. Naked Energy's primary focus is large commercial buildings, hotels, and hospitals, with a notable installation at the British Library in London (950 collectors, 700 sq meters, reducing carbon emissions by 55 tons/year). Naked Energy launched its first US project in late 2023 at Creighton University, with 240 collectors heating water for 400 students and reducing carbon emissions by 40 tons/year. Their US distribution partner, ELM Solar, is investing in a Dallas factory to manufacture these tubes domestically. Triple Solar, founded in 2009 in Amsterdam, focuses on residential applications. Their panels resemble traditional PV panels but have an open aluminum heat exchanger on the reverse side. This design allows them to capture heat from direct sunlight, diffused daylight, and the surrounding air (via convection), operating 24/7 down to -10°C. This cooling increases annual electricity generation by 5-10%. The system achieves over 70% total solar energy utilization and, when paired with a heat pump, a seasonal coefficient of performance of up to 5.6 for heating. A key advantage is its silent operation, eliminating the need for noisy outdoor heat pump units, which is crucial in densely populated European areas with strict noise regulations (e.g., 40 dB in Netherlands, 35 dB in Germany). Triple Solar partnered with German firm Consolar in 2016 and has installed systems in over 6,000 apartments in the Netherlands. Dutch pension giant ABP invested €10 million in Triple Solar in January 2023. The video explains why PVT adoption has been slow in the US: higher production costs due to complex manufacturing (vacuum seals, precise connections), weight (requiring structural reinforcement), and specialized installation (requiring both electrical and plumbing expertise). The main barrier is the lack of regulatory pressure seen in Europe, where noise restrictions and renewable energy mandates have made silent, efficient rooftop systems a necessity. The video suggests that PVT will follow the path of conventional solar, first scaling in commercial applications before becoming affordable for residential use, especially with the "commerce first, made in America" approach to navigate tax credits and import duties.
