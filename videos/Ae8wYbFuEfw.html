MIT Figured Out How Consumer LiDAR Can See Around Corners #mit #technology #technews

@octopusinc_ · 2026-06-01 · Favorites, Hardware, ListAssist

MIT Media Lab researchers have developed a method to enable consumer-grade LiDAR sensors, like those found in iPhones, to "see around corners" using non-line-of-sight (NLOS) imaging. This technique, which previously required expensive lab equipment, utilizes an algorithm called motion-induced aperture sampling to process faint, multi-bounce light signals from hidden objects, making it possible to reconstruct 3D shapes and track moving targets with inexpensive hardware. The code has been open-sourced, opening up applications in robotics, autonomous vehicles, and AR/VR.

The core innovation is an algorithm called "motion-induced aperture sampling." LiDAR works by firing laser pulses and measuring time-of-flight. While direct reflections are typically registered, this method leverages faint, multi-bounce signals where light scatters off a wall, hits a hidden object, bounces back to the wall, and then to the sensor. The algorithm addresses the challenge of this "chaotic mess of data points" by treating natural hand jitter or robot vibration as an asset, similar to how burst photography stacks noisy frames or satellites use synthetic aperture radar. It models the object's shape, motion, and camera position over time into a unified multi-frame measurement model, enforcing temporal coherence to strip away noise and extract the signal. The results are significant for a sensor with roughly 100 pixels, allowing for 3D reconstruction of hidden static objects, tracking multiple moving targets out of view, and real-time camera localization using hidden landmarks.
