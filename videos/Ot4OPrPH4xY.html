The Rise of CaaS: Context-as-a-Service for Agentic AI — Omer Primor, Bright Data

@octopusinc_ · 2026-08-14 · DevOps, Up Next

This video compares different methods for retrieving web context for AI agents, specifically focusing on "Context-as-a-Service" (CaaS) vendors, AI search products, and a "build-it-yourself" (DIY) scraping pipeline. The speaker, Omer Primor from Bright Data, presents a test where 25 fields for 100 companies are enriched, analyzing coverage and cost. The key takeaway is that while CaaS and AI search are convenient for ad-hoc tasks, owning the data pipeline becomes significantly more cost-effective for frequent, recurring queries, with a tipping point around 15,000 queries.

The presentation compares AI search, Context-as-a-Service (CaaS) vendors, and a DIY scraping pipeline for enriching company profiles with 25 fields. A test involving 100 companies and Claude Opus 4.8 as the agent harness was conducted. In terms of coverage, general AI search solutions (Search 1, Search 2, Search 3, Unlocker + SERP, Native) performed consistently well, often achieving 20-25 fields out of 25. CaaS vendors (CaaS 1, CaaS 2, CaaS 3) showed more variability, with some scoring lower (e.g., CaaS 2 and CaaS 3 often below 15 fields) because they are limited to their existing data, unlike search which can explore. Cost analysis per company showed CaaS 2 as the cheapest at $0.17, followed by Search 1, Search 2, and Unlocker + SERP around $0.34-$0.36. Native search was expensive at $1.30, and CaaS 3 was the most expensive at $1.50. The DIY solution, built in about a day using Bright Data's Scraper Studio and existing scrapers for sources like LinkedIn and Crunchbase, achieved a cost of $0.02 per company, excluding a $5,000 setup cost for a week's work. The "tipping point" where DIY becomes cheaper than renting was estimated to be around 15,000 queries/entities, after which the upfront investment in DIY is recouped. For high-frequency, recurring tasks, owning the context pipeline offers compounding value and avoids the "silent killer" of repeated query costs, token burn, and scope cutting associated with rented solutions.
