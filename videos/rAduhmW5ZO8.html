Why This Mars Battery Could Beat Lithium-Ion

@octopusinc_ · 2026-05-26 · Hardware

This video introduces Noon Energy's ultra-long duration energy storage system, a technology derived from NASA's MOXIE project on Mars. The system captures CO2, splits it into carbon and oxygen, and stores energy in solid carbon, offering a potential solution for grid-scale energy storage that can last for days, unlike typical lithium-ion batteries. It aims to complement, rather than replace, lithium-ion batteries by filling the multi-day backup gap currently met by natural gas peaker plants.

This system addresses the critical need for multi-day energy storage to support renewable energy grids, a gap that traditional lithium-ion batteries (2-10 hours) cannot fill. Noon Energy's system demonstrated over 100 hours of stored energy from a single shipping container-sized unit. The core process involves capturing CO2 from the atmosphere, splitting it into solid carbon and oxygen using electricity, and storing the energy in the carbon. During discharge, the carbon is reoxidized in a solid oxide fuel cell, recombining with oxygen to release electricity and CO2, with no net carbon release over a full cycle. This approach uses less than 1% of the critical materials required for lithium-ion batteries, making it potentially more affordable and scalable. The system has three main components: a power block, a charge tank, and a discharge tank. The separation of power blocks from charge tanks allows for more affordable scaling. Noon Energy claims a levelized cost of storage (LCOS) of around $0.05/kWh for 100-hour duration, significantly lower than lithium-ion's $1.20/kWh for the same duration. While its round-trip efficiency (60-80%) is lower than lithium-ion (85-95%), the lower installation and operational costs make it competitive, similar to pumped hydro. The system is designed to complement lithium-ion batteries for long-term storage, not replace them for short-duration needs.
