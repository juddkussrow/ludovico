We Open Sourced World Generation

@octopusinc_ · 2026-08-25 · Favorites, Tutorial, Up Next, Video Gen

This video demonstrates a workflow for generating explorable 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes from a single image or text prompt, locally and for free, using custom ComfyUI nodes. The core pipeline leverages Matrix-3D and Wan 2.1 to hallucinate missing scene details and allows users to "pilot" a virtual drone to define camera paths for synthetic dataset creation. The output is a standard COLMAP dataset, compatible with various Gaussian Splatting trainers like Brush.

The process begins with either generating a 360-degree image using a custom Krea 2 LoRA or uploading an existing one. This initial image is then processed to fix seams and upscale to 8K resolution. The core of the workflow involves using Matrix-3D, which takes the 360-degree panorama, estimates its depth, and creates a rough 3D mesh. A virtual drone is then piloted through this mesh in ComfyUI's custom path editor, defining camera trajectories. The video model Wan 2.1, enhanced with a Matrix-3D LoRA, hallucinates missing scene details along these paths, generating 720p video. To overcome the low resolution of Wan 2.1, the original 8K panorama is reprojected onto the generated video using camera data, resulting in sharper output. The workflow also integrates LightX2V for speedup and offers optional Sage Attention and Triton for further optimization, though a specific patch is required for Sage Attention to prevent black frames. The final output is a COLMAP dataset, which can be used with various Gaussian Splatting trainers. The video demonstrates training a splat using Brush, an open-source tool, showing how the quality varies based on the drone's coverage. Earlier attempts with Apple SHARP, UniSHARP, HY-World 2.0, and NVIDIA Lyra were discussed and discarded due to limitations like lack of flexibility, license restrictions, high resource demands (four GPUs for HY-World 2.0, 91GB checkpoints and Linux-only for Lyra), or falling apart when moving. Marble by World Labs was noted for its speed and crisp results but had restricted movement and lacked reflections.
