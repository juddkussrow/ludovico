Bee is described as a highly sensitive capture device, recording approximately 10 million tokens per year per user, capable of learning "virtually everything" about a person within a week. The core mission is to ensure no one, including Amazon, can access user data. This is achieved through a "Security by Design" approach with four principles: customer-only access, encryption by default, transparency and auditability, and a minimal trust base. Key management is central: the encryption key is generated and stored only on the user's device (iPhone or Android) and never leaves it. Bee never stores the key. Before data is handed over, the phone runs an attestation pipeline that verifies the workload against a public transparency log, Sigstore, allowing anyone to verify the code's authenticity. Inference runs on Bee's own models within confidential compute environments. Keys in memory expire after seven days to limit exposure. A separate Amazon privacy team holds the signing keys, hardcoded into the apps, preventing Bee's team from shipping unnoticed changes. The system is built with about 20,000 lines of memory-safe code, primarily for attestation verification, avoiding homegrown crypto. The system is designed for a stateful personal AI that runs continuously, proactively acts on the user's behalf, and does not require the user's device to be online.
