He defines video as a "spatial temporal volume" that is temporal, multimodal, dense, ambiguous, and expensive to process. TwelveLabs' solution is a video intelligence stack comprising semantic chunks, a multimodal embedding encoder called Marengo, a spatio-temporal context store, and a video context-aware language model named Pegasus. This stack is exposed as an API. The key concept is the "context graph," a durable, queryable representation connecting time-bounded moments, entities, appearances, relationships, and corpus-level context. Five principles guide its construction: ingest once and reason many times (like a database), store primitives (moments, entities, appearances) not just answers, ground every claim to a timestamp, let intent shape memory (e.g., brand safety vs. sports highlights), and keep the memory layer composable (API-first). He contrasts "search" (finding moments) with "memory" (preserving meaning and enabling structured knowledge, timelines, and composable outputs). A "video worker" is introduced as a deterministic system with capabilities like memory, task planning, retrieval, expert tools, operating envelopes, output contracts, and evaluation. Demos of their product, Jockey, include analyzing 67 World Cup videos to find "near misses" (shots that almost scored), tracking Messi across the corpus, and analyzing traffic security footage (counting vehicles, detecting safety events) and ad placement opportunities in brand videos. Jockey is positioned as a "video cognition infrastructure" rather than an application, providing a knowledge store, configurable ingestion, corpus digests, and resolution-as-you-search APIs.
