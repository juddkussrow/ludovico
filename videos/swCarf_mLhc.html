He starts by collecting insects from his garden, then prepares them by freezing and rehydrating them in a steam bath to allow for posing. The capture rig involves a camera on a focus rail, with the subject rotating. This setup allows for focus stacking, where multiple images at different depths are combined into one fully in-focus image per angle. He initially used a setup that took 4-5 hours per insect, but upgraded to a Nikon Z8 camera with continuous LED lighting, enabling 10 frames per second shooting in 8K, reducing capture time to about half an hour per insect. The raw images (JPEGs) are then processed using Helicon Focus for focus stacking. The stacked photos are then masked using a chroma key (red background) and tracked in Colmap to reconstruct camera positions. For reconstruction, he primarily uses Postshot, noting its efficiency with memory and ability to handle up to 5 million splats, even with a graphics card with only 8GB of VRAM. He has also experimented with Brush and Luma AI, and plans to use Luma AI Studio more with a more powerful graphics card. He prefers MCMC in Postshot for its larger splats that fill gaps, while Splat 3 tends to create holes, though Splat 3 is more reliable when MCMC fails. He advises against using a turntable for smaller subjects and emphasizes the importance of ample, well-thought-out lighting. He showcases impressive splats of a carpenter bee (3.2 million splats) and a field grasshopper, noting challenges with transparency in darker subjects. He has explored viewing these splats in VR using the Tiango platform on a Quest 2, but found the design and conceptualization of interactive experiences to be the main hurdle, rather than the headset's capability.
