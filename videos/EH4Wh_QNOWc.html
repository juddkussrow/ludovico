Moire disc - 3D printed kinetic sculpture

@octopusinc_ · 2026-08-21 · Hardware

This video details the creation of a 3D-printed kinetic sculpture that generates interference patterns by rotating two discs. The creator designed a custom PCB to control two modified 28byj-48 stepper motors using an ESP32C6 microcontroller and TMC2209 drivers. A web-based simulator and Inkscape were used for disc pattern design, with the final parts 3D printed and assembled into a wall-mounted art piece.

The creator initially explored existing designs, including an Instructable by Dushyant Ahuja, but found them unsuitable for his desired compact, wall-mounted, quiet, and continuously running design. His first prototype used DC motors and nested shafts, but he disliked the DC motors and varied bearing types. The second prototype, which is the focus of the video, switched to modified 28byj-48 stepper motors for better speed control and used four identical 608 bearings. A custom PCB was designed to integrate an ESP32C6 microcontroller and TMC2209 drivers, along with a DC buck converter. The creator developed a 3D-printed jig to simplify the modification of unipolar 28byj-48 stepper motors into bipolar ones by cutting a trace. The assembly involves mounting motors, a shaft with magnetic interfaces, and cable management using zip ties. For disc design, a web-based "Moire Simulator" was created, allowing users to draw patterns, animate them, and adjust instances. Designs were then exported as SVG, imported into Inkscape for replication and quartering, and finally into PrusaSlicer for 3D printing. The discs are assembled from four quarters with magnets and small circumference parts. A remote controller was built using two potentiometers, a button, and an LED, offering three modes: individual disc speed control, color and gradient control for an LED strip, and an automatic speed sweep mode. The automatic speed sweep mode has limitations due to play in the mechanism, possibly from the stepper motor's internal 1:64 reduction and 3D-printed gears introducing backlash.
