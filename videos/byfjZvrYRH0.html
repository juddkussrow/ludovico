ThinkingCap was trained not just for correctness but for efficient correctness, rewarding the model for stopping its reasoning process once confident in an answer. This approach resulted in an average 45.8% reduction in thinking tokens across 12 out-of-domain benchmarks, with accuracy barely changing (less than one percentage point decrease). On in-domain benchmarks, token reduction was nearly 58%, and on GSM 8K, accuracy actually increased from 93.3% to 96.5%. The model also significantly reduced "looping" behavior, where models get stuck rephrasing the same reasoning. A live test on an RTX 5090 with 64GB RAM compared the base Qwen3.6-27B against ThinkingCap using the prompt "What is the smallest positive integer n such that n! has exactly 100 trailing zeros?". The base Qwen model took 140 seconds and 7653 thinking tokens to arrive at the correct answer (405), with a total of 8567 tokens. ThinkingCap, in contrast, completed the task in 29.89 seconds, using only 1117 thinking tokens and 1864 total tokens, while also providing the correct answer (405). ThinkingCap also achieved a slightly faster token per second speed (62 vs 61.1). A notable "bug" during development, which accidentally shortened the final answer text in addition to the thinking trace, was kept because internal testers preferred the more concise output, theorizing that humans also prefer shorter, more direct answers. ThinkingCap is available on Hugging Face under the Apache 2.0 license.
