Free cloud solutions: MipMap3D (1GB, 19.3M splats, free up to 500 images) struggled with alignment, resulting in broken reconstructions. Aholo3D (440MB, 1.9M splats, free for basic use) produced solid but ghosted results with sky trapped as a dome. Free Gaussian (1.2GB, 5M splats, free) had more ghosting and transparency, requiring users to grant a permanent worldwide license for data usage. In a direct comparison, Aholo3D generally outperformed Free Gaussian. Paid cloud solutions: Splatica (1.7GB, 7.8M splats, $0.16/second) delivered very solid reconstructions with good detail retention, though reflective surfaces like water were tricky. Scaniverse (866MB, 3.6M splats, $0.016/second) showed some environment and sky trapped but good detail in other areas. Captures Studio (133MB, 1.5M splats, $0.04/second, 2 free credits) produced solid results in well-lit areas but struggled with dark areas and trapped sky. Spatial Studio (42MB SOG, 3M splats, $0.06/second) offered more control over quality settings and produced a very solid, sharp reconstruction with impressive water, making it a top contender. Side-by-side, Spatial Studio generally outperformed Splatica, especially in dark areas and detail clarity, despite color tone differences. Local solutions: PostShot (690MB, 3M splats, €26/month for unlimited local reconstruction) produced impressive results with no transparency over bricks and clear details, though water was still messy. LichtFeld (726MB, 3M splats, free) used a modified pipeline with direct fisheye support, resulting in crisp and color-accurate output, surpassing cloud solutions, but still showing some transparency in dark areas. The creator also mentioned Brush, Polycam, and Luma AI as other local options. The local processing was done on a Dell Pro Max Tower T2 with an NVIDIA RTX 6000. Overall, local solutions like PostShot and LichtFeld demonstrated superior quality compared to cloud-based options, with Spatial Studio being the strongest among the paid cloud services.
