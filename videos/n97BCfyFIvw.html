This video, a keynote by Addy Osmani, discusses the evolving role of software engineers in the age of AI agents. He argues that as AI automates coding tasks, engineers' value shifts from code production to accountability, judgment, and system ownership. The core message is that engineers must focus on "answerability" and "taste" to remain effective and accountable, rather than succumbing to cognitive debt or surrender.

He emphasizes that while AI automates coding, the engineer's value shifts to "answerability," which involves owning the evidence, understanding, and verdict of increasingly automated work. He defines quality as the system of checks producing evidence, verdict as the human/accountable decision from that evidence, and answerability as the ability to explain and stand behind the verdict. Osmani highlights that roles are rebundling around ownership, moving beyond craft boundaries. He introduces the concept of an "agentic software factory," where agents handle the inner loop (generate, verify, solve) and humans make production decisions (ship, block, redirect). He notes that AI-generated code is becoming normal, making answerability an engineering requirement. Clean code is beneficial for agents, leading to 7-8% fewer tokens and 34% fewer file revisits, with the same pass rate. However, generation scales faster than review, creating a "governance gap" where review/validation becomes a bottleneck (85% of organizations), and AI code introduces new technical debt (82%). He warns against "cognitive debt" (erosion of understanding due to AI reliance) and "cognitive surrender" (blindly accepting AI's responses, with a Wharton study showing 73% accepted wrong AI answers and felt more sure). He also mentions "orchestration tax," the cognitive drain from managing many parallel AI agents. Osmani argues that "taste" and "judgment" are crucial skills that decay slower than speed, recall, or verification. He defines taste as the ability to make high-quality qualitative judgments where no objective metric exists yet. The "loop boundary is evidence," with agents running the inner capability loop (investigate, implement, test, report) and engineers owning the outer agency loop (decide, verify, approve, own). The operational rule is "explain it or don't ship it," meaning someone must understand the work well enough to defend it. He concludes that automation moves the floor, but engineering moves up a level, focusing on loop design, evidence design, and brownfield stewardship.
