LatchBio, initially a data tool vendor for biotech and pharma, shifted to building AI agents that use large language models (LLMs) to steer analysis with natural language. They found that while early agent prototypes showed promise, frontier models like Sonnet 4.5 could not yet be trusted for real work due to their inability to extract scientific insight from messy, real-world data, struggling with programming, data analysis, and domain reasoning. To address this, LatchBio developed benchmarks like SpatialBench, which includes 146 problems spanning various kits and tasks in spatial biology. These benchmarks emphasize verifiability (checking success with a Python function), durability (conclusions robust across analysis paths), and anti-shortcut (requiring interaction with data, not memorized knowledge). They also created SpatialBench-Long for more complex, long-horizon tasks that simulate entire paper result sections or drug program decisions, acknowledging that these often require human verification due to the lack of clear ground truth in science. LatchBio also introduced TxBench-PP for preclinical pharmacology and BioSecBench-Refusal to evaluate model refusals in biosecurity contexts, distinguishing between routine and red-team tasks. The company notes that models like Claude Opus 4.8, Fable, and Sonnet 5 have adopted their benchmarks. They observe that models perform drastically better on routine tasks than red-team tasks in biosecurity.
