We Just Hit the Local LLM Tipping Point (Colibri)

@octopusinc_ · 2026-07-13 · DevOps

This video introduces Colibri, a 1,300-line C engine that enables running the 744 billion parameter GLM-5.2 LLM on a laptop with only 25GB of RAM and no GPU. It achieves this by exploiting the Mixture of Experts (MoE) architecture of GLM-5.2, where only a small fraction of parameters are active per token. Colibri pins the frequently used "dense core" in RAM and streams the remaining 21,504 experts from the SSD on demand, significantly outperforming `llama.cpp`'s memory-mapping approach for MoE models.

GLM-5.2, an open model from Z.ai, is a Mixture of Experts (MoE) model. Its weights, at 4-bit precision, total 370 GB. Colibri leverages the MoE architecture by recognizing that only about 40 billion parameters (5%) are active per token. It pins a 10GB "dense core" (attention layers, embeddings, one shared expert) in RAM. The remaining 21,504 experts (19 MB each), which constitute the 370 GB file, reside on the SSD. For each token, the router picks 8 experts. Colibri checks a RAM cache for these experts; if missing, it reads them directly from the SSD at their exact byte offset and evicts the least used expert. This process involves about 11 GB of disk reads for a fully cold token. Colibri maintains a usage log on disk to pin frequently used experts, making it faster with more use. This approach is contrasted with `llama.cpp`'s memory-mapping, which the video argues is less efficient for MoE models because the OS guesses which pages to load, blocking threads, and cannot predict future needs. Colibri, with its router, knows which experts are needed and requests them asynchronously, even pre-loading for the next layer with 72% accuracy. Performance benchmarks show Colibri running at 0.1 tokens/second on a budget SSD, 0.28 on a PCIe 5 drive, 0.37 on a 128GB Framework laptop, and 1 token/second on an M5 Max. The video clarifies that streaming reads do not wear out SSDs, unlike writes, though thermal throttling on cheap drives is a risk. Other projects like antirez's ds4 and `llama.cpp`'s expert-cache proposals are also exploring similar SSD-as-memory-tier approaches.
