Ardy, from Nvidia, is an autoregressive diffusion model for interactive human motion generation, supporting real-time 3D animation and humanoid robot control (Uni Tree G1). Its code and models are open-sourced and relatively small (765 MB per model). MobileWan allows local video generation on mobile devices, taking text prompts to output 5-second, 480p, 16fps videos in about 20 seconds on a phone. This is achieved through heavy optimization like chunk processing, attention head pruning, reduced generation steps (3 instead of 20-30), and a memory-efficient video decoder. The total size is 9.9 GB. Nvidia's PiD upscaler v1.5 offers improved details and color fidelity over v1, working with models like Quinn image, Flux, and Z image, and can upscale 512x512 images to 2K in seconds on a consumer GPU. Audio to MIDI by Mirell transcribes full songs into separate MIDI tracks for instruments and vocals, with a free online demo. It's open-source with small, medium, and large variants (large is 1.4B parameters, 5.5 GB), but model weights are non-commercial. WanDancer from Alibaba generates long, synchronized dance videos from music and an image, without needing reference movements. It's open-source but requires a high-end GPU due to its 85 GB size. Google's GNM (Generative Anthropometric Model) allows precise 3D face and head design with extensive controls (253 identity, 383 expression controls), released under Apache 2 license. Lucida is an open-source background remover (MIT license, <900 MB) that excels at preserving tricky details like glass, transparency, text, and hair, outperforming Ideogram in examples shown. Motion4motion transfers movement between videos without matching skeletons or ControlNet, by tracking points to create a motion flow map. Higgsfield MCP, a sponsor, integrates AI agents like Claude and ChatGPT for content creation, generating images, videos, and marketing campaigns. Bonsai 27B compresses Alibaba's Quen 3.627B model (originally 56 GB) to run on mobile phones, with a Ternary variant at 5.9 GB (95% accuracy) and a 1-bit variant at 3.9 GB (90% accuracy), fitting on an iPhone 17 Pro.
